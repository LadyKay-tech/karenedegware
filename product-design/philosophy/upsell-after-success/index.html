Principle

Upsell after success, not on a schedule.

Most upgrade prompts run on a timer, with a pop-up on day three and an email on day seven, so they arrive whenever the calendar says rather than when they would be welcome. More often than not, that means interrupting someone in the middle of their work.

A much better moment is straight after the product has helped, particularly when the person has just run into the very limit the upgrade removes. At that point the value is obvious to them, and the offer barely needs selling.

So I design these prompts around behaviour rather than dates. If someone has spent their Friday afternoon sending twenty payments by hand, that tells you exactly who needs bulk payouts and when to mention them, and the offer can speak to what they have just done using their own numbers.

Offers timed like this convert better and irritate far fewer people. When it is done well, the customer feels that the product has noticed what they need, which is a very different experience from being chased by it.
