Principle

Make leaving easy, and listen on the way out.

The way a product lets people go says a great deal about how confident it is. When the cancel button is hidden, the message to customers is fairly clear: we suspect you would leave if you could find the way out.

A maze of screens might hold on to a few people for one more month, but it also makes sure they never return, gives their friends a reason to stay away and, in a growing number of countries, breaks consumer law as well.

I would rather make cancelling as easy as signing up. There is one clear route out, a single honest question about why the person is leaving, and the option to pause for anyone who only needs a break. Their data and settings are kept, so coming back later is just as easy as leaving was.

Handled this way, the exit also becomes some of the most useful research a team can get. People tend to be at their most honest when they are on their way out, and a short reason from everyone who goes will usually tell you more than a year of satisfaction surveys.
