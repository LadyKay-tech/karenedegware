Principle

A sign-up is not activation.

When someone creates an account it is tempting to count that as a win, but all a sign-up really tells me is that a person was curious enough to try. It says nothing about whether they found anything worth coming back for.

The moment I actually design for comes later: the first time someone gets something useful out of the product, whether that is a report they can send to their manager or a number they did not have yesterday. That is what I mean by activation, and I work backwards from it whenever I plan a first experience.

Working backwards changes how you look at every screen in between. Each one has to earn its place, and if I cannot say why someone needs it before they have seen any value, I either remove it or move it to after that first win. The two versions of the prototype show the difference far better than I can describe it.

This matters to the business as much as to the person using it, because people who get value early tend to stay longer, start paying sooner and bring their colleagues with them. That is usually the point where good design starts to show up in the numbers.
